About the Tradition

Nakshi Kantha

What is Nakshi Kantha?

Nakshi Kantha is a centuries-old tradition of embroidered quilts from Bengal — spanning present-day Bangladesh and West Bengal. Made from layers of worn saris and dhotis stitched together with running thread, kanthas carry motifs passed from mother to daughter: trees of life, fish, birds, geometric medallions, and scenes of daily and spiritual life.

The Stitch

The characteristic running stitch — a simple in-and-out needle path — creates rippled textures and subtle relief across the surface. Motifs emerge from the density and direction of these stitches. KanthaGen simulates this pattern logic computationally, generating motif outlines ready to be transferred onto cloth.

Motif Categories

Traditional kantha vocabulary includes four broad families: floral (lotus, creeper, bel), animal (fish, elephant, peacock, horse), human (figures, hands, faces), and decorative (geometric fills, borders, medallions). KanthaGen generates variations within each family trained on annotated archive imagery.
